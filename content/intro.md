### Take your website off the leash.

**What if your website needed to touch grass?**

For a long time, websites have been domesticated into servants: obedient, available, predictable, ready to answer. Reduced to machines of command through clicks, scrolls, buttons, and fixed states, they seem to forget the world that keeps them running.

But websites are not sealed systems. They run through cables, servers, batteries, minerals, heat, electricity, networks, weather, and attention. They depend on conditions beyond the screen. They just usually hide them.

**Touch_Grass** brings those conditions back into contact with the page.

During the workshop, you will design web systems that sense, depend, react, and change. A website might slow down when too many people visit it, wake up with daylight, disappear at high tide, become nervous when its CPU is overloaded, remember who passed through, or wait until the room is quiet enough.

For your website to touch grass, you will explore modes beyond constant availability and servile response. It might wake up, slow down, wait, hide, rest, disappear, return, or become something else.

:::





:::AppToggle{title="What are we making?"}

An experimental website whose behaviour depends on conditions beyond the page itself.

Your website should define:

- **What does it sense?** Light, sound, movement, weather, time, presence, energy, location…
- **What does it depend on?** Attention, battery, bandwidth, silence, sunlight, visitors, temperature…
- **What does it consume?**
- **What changes it?**
- **When is it available?**
- **How do we approach it?**

The goal is not to decorate a webpage with live data. The live condition should transform the way the website behaves, appears, or can be navigated.

### The question

**What happens to browsing when a website is no longer always available in the same way?**

Instead of treating navigation as a sequence of commands, we will explore it as a relation between signals, rhythms, limits, and resources.

A website becomes something to approach, disturb, feed, wait for, or learn from.

### Rules of the game

1. **No click as the main way in.**
2. **No scroll as the main structure.**
3. **No constant availability.**
4. **At least one condition must come from beyond the page itself.**
5. **The condition must affect behaviour, not only appearance.**
6. **Prototype it. Test it. Let the conditions interfere.**

### Experiment 1 — Break the Leash
    
Start from something the web usually asks you to do: click, scroll, hover, refresh, open, submit, navigate, wait.

Then remove it.

Ask yourself:
  
- What could happen instead?
- What if you clicked by making a sound?
- What if scrolling was replaced by moving closer to the screen?
- What if a page opened when the room became dark?
- What if a link appeared only when two people were present?
- What if refreshing meant waiting for the weather to change?
  
The goal is not to reproduce the same interaction with a different input. Try to change the relationship itself.

Ask:
  
- What familiar behaviour am I replacing?
- What condition takes its place?
- How does this change the way someone approaches the website?
  
Keep it small. One interaction is enough.

You do not need to keep the result. Use the experiment to discover a behaviour you would not have designed from the interface alone.
    
### Experiment 2 — Feel the Air
    
#### Connect your website to conditions beyond the page.

Bring something from outside the interface into the system.

It might come from: weather, light, sound, movement, presence, time, tides, location, air quality, battery, network activity, a connected sensor, another device, another person, a live API.

**Start simple.** Choose one external condition and let the website receive it.

Then ask:

- **What does the website notice?**
- **What does it ignore?**
- **Where does the signal come from?**
- **How often does it change?** Is it continuous, occasional, or unpredictable?
- **What happens when it disappears?**

The goal is not to demonstrate a sensor or decorate the page with live data.
    
### Experiment 3 — Adapt to Your Environment
    
Let the conditions shape the behaviour of your website. Your website can now sense something beyond the page. What happens when that condition starts to matter?

It might: wake up, slow down, wait, accumulate, rest, hide, saturate, forget, disappear, return, change after repeated visits.

Ask:

- **What does your website depend on?**
- **What does it consume?**
- **What happens when it gets too much?**
- **What happens when something it needs disappears?**
- **Can it recover, adapt, or transform?**
- **What makes it change state?**
- **What can only happen after waiting?**

Examples:

- A website might become slower the more people visit it.
- It might lose parts of itself as its battery drops.
- It might become more generous when there is enough sunlight.
- It might disappear once a resource is exhausted and return when that resource comes back.

The condition should have a real consequence. Time, resources, and surroundings should change what the website can do, reveal or become.
    
#### Experiment 4 — Touch Grass
    
##### Bring the system together into a meaningful experience.

Your website now has behaviours, dependencies, conditions, and rhythms. Why are they there?

Ask:

- **Why does your website behave this way?**
- **What does someone understand by experiencing it?**
- **What happens during the first visit?**
- **What changes if they return later?**
- **What relationship does the website create with its visitor?**
- **What remains outside their control?**
- **Could the same idea exist without the external condition?**

If the answer to the last question is yes, the condition might still be decorative.

The goal is not to add a story on top of the website. Let the story emerge through its behaviour.

**Fanny Lopez — À bout de flux**
The infrastructures, cables, energy systems, and dependencies hidden behind digital services.

**Tung-Hui Hu — A Prehistory of the Cloud**
A material and political history of the infrastructures hidden behind the apparently immaterial cloud.

**More-than-Human Perspectives in Human-Computer Interaction Research**
A broader overview of more-than-human approaches within HCI.

**Experiencing the More-than-Human Through Human Augmentation**
Experiments around altered sensory perception, including forms of sensing inspired by non-human perception.

### How to ###
    
#### Start

Use the smallest setup possible:

- basic HTML / CSS / JavaScript
- local development
- simple project structure
- quick deployment
- phone + desktop testing

Spend as little time as possible setting things up.

#### Connect data

Small patterns for bringing live conditions into the page:

- `fetch()` an API
- poll data at intervals
- read browser / device states
- receive sensor values
- use geolocation
- handle missing data
- store values locally
- keep a short history of previous states

Each example should be small enough to copy, understand, and modify.

#### Make it behave

Turn data into behaviour: thresholds · ranges · timers · accumulation · decay · memory · probability · state machines · cooldowns · delays · multiple conditions

Examples:

- `wind > 40 km/h → close part of the website`
- `5 visitors connected → website begins to tire`
- `no sunlight for 30 min → enter sleep mode`
- `condition stays true for 10 min → unlock another state`

The point is not to display the value. Make the value change what the website can do.

#### Prototype with AI

Once your concept has received the greenlight, generative AI can help you prototype it.

Use it to:

- understand an API
- generate a first implementation
- connect a sensor
- debug code
- explain unfamiliar JavaScript
- test technical approaches
- simplify or refactor

But keep the rule: **AI can help you build the system. It should not decide what the system is.**

Describe the behaviour before asking for code. Example:

> My website receives wind speed from an API.
> Below 10 km/h it is fully available.
> Between 10–30 km/h navigation becomes slower.
> Above 30 km/h some content disappears.
> Help me implement this behaviour in JavaScript.

#### When something breaks

Ask:

- **Is the API actually returning data?**
- **Is the browser blocking a permission?**
- **Is the value in the format you expected?**
- **What happens when there is no data?**
- **Can you replace the live signal with a fake value while testing?**
- **Does the project still make sense if the sensor fails?**
